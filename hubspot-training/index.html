Why This Training Is Different

The gap in most HubSpot training isn't knowledge of the interface, it's that the trainer has never had to make the portal do real work. I have. I built and operate NetworkDays, a HubSpot actual business days calculator with a real OAuth install, write custom-coded workflow actions, and architected RevOps systems during a PE-backed three-company merger. So when your team asks "can HubSpot actually do this?", the answer is grounded in what the platform can genuinely deliver, not a sales deck. That's the difference between training that fades and training that changes how a team works.

A Recent Example

HubSpot's Agent Hub launched July 23, 2026. By mid-September, I had already run a live session for a client's admins and COO covering how Agent Hub actually works, how to build agents that support their own workflows, and how to read credit spend so agent usage has a real ROI answer instead of a guess. Most training closes that gap months late: by the time a generic course exists for a brand-new feature, a team has already spent weeks forming workarounds blind.

Sales Hub Training

The outcome that actually matters to sales leaders and execs: a team that moves faster inside the sales workspace, and a HubSpot subscription that earns back its license spend instead of sitting half-used. Reps learn to build a deal pipeline that matches their real sales process instead of HubSpot's default stages, set up sequences and task queues that fit the team's actual cadence, and read a forecast that reflects what is genuinely in play. We cover deal rotting, stage-exit criteria, and how to use custom objects when deals alone cannot represent the business (multi-location accounts, renewal cycles, usage-based deals). A pipeline the team trusts enough to keep updated is also the pipeline that justifies what the company pays for every seat.

Marketing Hub Training

Marketing teams learn lead scoring that reflects real buying signals instead of a generic point system nobody revisits, workflow design for nurture and lifecycle stage changes, and campaign reporting that ties spend back to pipeline rather than stopping at form submissions. We also cover attribution reporting, including where HubSpot's default source data breaks down and how to fix it, a gap documented in detail in this site's own attribution write-up.

Service Hub Training

Service teams learn to structure ticket pipelines around actual resolution paths, configure SLAs that reflect real response commitments instead of HubSpot's defaults, and build a knowledge base that actually deflects the tickets it exists to handle. We also cover customer health reporting that connects support activity back to the same CRM data sales and marketing use, so a renewal risk shows up before it becomes a lost account, not after.

Operations Hub and the CRM Data Layer

This is where most HubSpot training stops short, and where this training spends the most time. Teams learn the custom object and association model underneath every hub, programmable automation (custom-coded workflow actions, not just the drag-and-drop defaults), data sync and deduplication logic, and the governance habits that keep a data model clean as a team scales. The goal across every hub above is the same: self-sufficiency, so the team can maintain what gets built here without ongoing external help.
