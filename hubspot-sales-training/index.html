HubSpot Sales Team Training for Reps and Managers

Sales Hub training for reps and sales managers, run in your portal and built around how your team actually sells.

Your company bought Sales Hub seats and the team still works out of spreadsheets, inboxes and memory. A rep can create a deal, but the pipeline does not match how the team sells, stages mean different things to different people, and the forecast is a guess. I train sales teams on their own portal so reps know what to update, managers know what to read, and managers stop chasing updates by hand.

Who this is for

This is for a team that already has Sales Hub and needs it to become the place the work happens.

  • A sales team whose seats are half used, with deals updated only when a manager asks.
  • Reps and SDRs who need a cadence they can keep, rather than another tab to check.
  • Sales managers who want a forecast built from stage data instead of optimism.
  • Sales ops or RevOps staff who maintain the pipeline for everyone else.
  • A team that has just moved from another CRM, or from Starter to Professional or Enterprise.

What the team learns

The agenda follows your sales process. These are the areas most sales teams need first.

  1. Pipeline and deal stages. Rebuilding stages to match how the team actually sells, with exit criteria for each one, rather than the default labels that came with the account.
  2. Deal hygiene. Which properties are required at each stage, how close dates are set, and how a stalled deal surfaces on the board before the forecast review.
  3. Sequences and task queues. A cadence reps can keep, with tasks that appear on the day they are due and a queue that clears in one sitting.
  4. Forecasting. How the forecast rolls up from stages, owners and close dates, and which settings quietly distort the total.
  5. Manager reporting. Dashboards by rep, stage and source that a sales manager can read before a Monday meeting.
  6. Custom objects, where your subscription includes them and deals are not enough. Multi-location accounts, renewal cycles or usage-based contracts that a single deal record cannot represent.
  7. Contact and company records. When to create a company, how to associate it to a deal, and which fields reps should leave alone.

How sessions run

Every engagement starts with a short scoping call. I look at your pipeline, ask how the team sells and where the handoffs break, then build an agenda around what is missing rather than a standard syllabus.

Sessions are live and remote. A whole team usually takes a half-day or full-day intensive, and a larger team is better served by role-specific workshops across several weeks. Managers often get a session of their own on pipeline inspection and forecast review, since their work in HubSpot differs from a rep's.

View access to your portal helps me prepare with real examples. If access is restricted, training can run on screen share only. Every session is recorded on request, and the work is quoted at a fixed price after scoping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you train managers as well as reps?

Yes. Managers usually get a session of their own on pipeline inspection, forecast review and dashboards, because their job in HubSpot differs from a rep's. Reps get the hands-on work.

Do you need access to our portal?

View access helps me prepare with your real pipeline and properties. Training can also run on screen share only if access is restricted.

Is this the same as HubSpot Academy?

No. Academy teaches the product in general. This teaches your team your own portal, with your stages, your sequences and your reporting.

How many sessions does a sales team need?

It depends on how many roles are in the room and how much of the pipeline needs rebuilding. The scoping call sets the number before anything is quoted. I check your Sales Hub tier during scoping, since some tools depend on it.

Will reps keep using the CRM after the sessions?

The format is built around habits a manager can inspect: required fields at each stage, a task queue tied to the cadence, and a dashboard reviewed in the weekly meeting.
