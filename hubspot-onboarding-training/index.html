HubSpot Onboarding Training for Teams, Week by Week

Onboarding for a team that is new to HubSpot, or for new hires joining a portal that already runs.

A one-day workshop feels productive, then fades. People leave with notes, a demo portal and no practice, and three weeks later the same questions come back. Onboarding works better spaced out: a short session, a week of real work in the portal, another session that answers what came up. I run that program for teams new to HubSpot and for new hires joining a portal that already runs, with sessions built for each role instead of one agenda for everyone.

Who this is for

Most teams arrive here at one of two moments: after an implementation, when the portal is configured but the habits are not, or during a run of hiring, when new people need to learn the house rules quickly.

  • A company that has just implemented HubSpot and needs the team to use it properly.
  • New hires joining a portal that already has pipelines, properties and templates.
  • A team moving from another CRM, where every screen is new.
  • Managers who want one shared baseline across roles.
  • An admin who has been asked to run onboarding internally and wants a structure to follow.

What the team learns

The program starts with the portal, not a product tour. Everything below is taught on your records, your properties and your naming.

  1. Portal orientation. The objects that matter to each role, how records connect, and where one team's work appears to another.
  2. Role tracks. Reps on deals, tasks and sequences; marketers on contacts, forms and campaigns; service on tickets and the inbox; managers on dashboards and handoffs.
  3. Data entry rules. Required properties, naming conventions, lifecycle stages and the small set of fields that keep reporting honest.
  4. Daily habits. The morning check, the task queue, the note standard, and what the portal logs without anyone typing.
  5. Working with what exists. The pipelines, properties and templates the company already set up, so a new person follows the house rules instead of inventing their own.

How sessions run

Sessions are short, live and remote, spaced across several weeks. Each one opens with what happened in the portal since the last session, then covers one topic, then ends with a small piece of real work to bring back. That week of practice is where people learn the portal.

Cohorts can be split by role, so reps do not sit through a service queue demonstration. The first orientation session often runs with everyone, because the shared picture helps, and the role sessions follow.

Sessions are recorded on request. The program is scoped on a short call: how many people, which roles, which hubs, and how much the portal already does. Pricing is fixed after that call.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an onboarding program take?

Programs are spaced over several weeks rather than delivered as one long workshop. The scoping call sets the number of sessions from your roles, your hubs and how much the team already knows.

Is this for new hires or for a team that is new to HubSpot?

Both. New hires get a track that covers your portal's rules and where their work lands. A team new to HubSpot gets a broader program that starts with the objects and ends with the reports each role owns.

Can we mix roles in one session?

For the first orientation, yes, because the shared picture helps. After that I split by role so each group spends its time on the work it actually does.

Do you need access to our portal?

View access helps me prepare with real records. Training can also run on screen share only if access is restricted.

What do participants keep afterwards?

Recordings on request, plus the real work each person did in your portal between sessions, which stays there after the program ends.
